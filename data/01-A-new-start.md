# A New Start 

After years without any activity here, I am starting to seriously explore that weird but productive space between anthropology/sociology, design, and digital methods again. I got tired of my [blog](https://sorrego.net) and wanted to try something different, something better aligned with how I understand multimodal-digital anthropology should be. Still, this is not a critique of anything other than my own work. The issue lies in an ontological misalignment: Multimodality has become a representation rather than a generative force for improving my ethnographic practice.

## Unpacking the Problem
I took an easy route by building my main blog. It was not a bad one; just an easy one, so easy that it became a boring path: using a content management system to display text, pictures, and illustrations. I brag about being someone interested in experimental methods; even my GitHub profile says I am an "experimental field methods designer." Of course, I have designed some methods and devices to ethnographically explore urban spaces and more-than-human entanglements, but all of them—despite some having been digitally made—were made only for analog work, and even though a few came back to the "digital world," this realm was used as a passive display structure. 

This matches a different moment in a different time: the early days of digital journalism, when digital was just an exact copy of the printed newspaper, perhaps adding more pictures and one video or audio clip. And it is not just about the structure but the format: the same way to write, the same reading logic, the same conceptual strategies. 

Back then, it was easier to understand why the internet was in its early days and why the whole industry was a bit nervous about change. Now, after so much work has been done to stabilize multimodal anthropology as a prominent and fruitful realm, there are no excuses for why someone like me is not taking full advantage of digital methods beyond the discipline to improve the discipline itself.

## About this Space
And here is where this GitHub space makes sense. I am not a designer, nor a programmer, nor someone with strong knowledge of digital literacy, but I am learning as this profile—its repositories, projects, and artifacts—grows. That learning path is also what I want to share here. I think it might serve as first-hand data on how to enhance ethnography digitally, and maybe it can help other colleagues interested in this matter. 

I do not know, however, how detailed I will be in the technicalities. I am not hiding anything or obscuring this process; I just think it may be hard to read for someone without prior knowledge of coding, design, or something in between. All this content is for anthropologists and related-field practitioners. I hope one day this space becomes a guide for social science students and practitioners interested in multimodal and experimental research.
